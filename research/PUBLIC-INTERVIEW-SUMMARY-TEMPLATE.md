# Public Interview Summary Template

**Purpose:** Give the "we talked to real people" claim something defensible to stand on.
Right now no interview log exists (see `SOURCES.md`, S13). Until each conversation is
recorded in a structure like this, the demand evidence in `FOOD-CHECKER-DESIGN.md` is an
assertion, not documented evidence, and should be described accordingly.

**This is a blank template. It contains no real people and no invented ones.** Do not
paste in personas that were not actually spoken to.

## Rules for using it

- **No identifiable information.** No names, no employers, no locations finer than a
  region, no photos, no contact details, nothing that could re-identify a person.
- **Anonymise at capture, not later.** Assign each person a code (P01, P02, ...) and use
  only the code.
- **Distinguish quote from paraphrase.** Put verbatim words in quotation marks; mark
  everything else as paraphrase. Never upgrade a paraphrase into a quotation.
- **Record consent.** Note that the person agreed their anonymised, paraphrased input can
  be used in a portfolio case study. If consent is unclear, use the row for internal
  reasoning only and do not publish it.
- **No clinical framing.** Record the person's stated constraint in their words. Do not
  diagnose, and do not present any of this as medical evidence.
- **One structural pattern.** The case study's argument is that these are one mechanism
  (translating a generic evaluation into a personal constraint under time pressure), not
  many separate problems. The final column is where you note how each conversation maps to
  that mechanism, so the generalisation is evidenced rather than asserted.

## Method note (fill in before publishing)

- **How people were reached:** (for example, informal conversations within personal
  network). Be accurate. If they were informal, call them informal.
- **How many conversations in total, and how many are logged here:** state both. Do not
  cite a number you cannot evidence.
- **Selection bias, stated plainly:** these were not a representative sample. Say so. The
  evidence supports "this friction is real and recurs across different constraints," not
  "this friction is prevalent at rate X."
- **What the interviews can and cannot support:** they can illustrate the mechanism and
  its varied contexts; they cannot establish market size, prevalence, or willingness to
  pay.

## Honest one-liner for the case study

> The demand evidence is a set of [N] informal, anonymised conversations across different
> dietary constraints. They are used to illustrate a shared decision-friction mechanism,
> not to estimate how common it is.
