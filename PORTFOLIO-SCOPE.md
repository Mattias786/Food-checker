## Purpose of this document

This scopes the employer-facing portfolio website that will host the Food-Checker
case study — what it contains, what it deliberately excludes, and what still has to
be true before publication. It does not contain case-study copy. It is the brief a
designer or a second reviewer would use to check the finished site against, and the
gate this project must clear before a single word of the public write-up is drafted.

## 1. Central proposition

The site's job is to prove one thing to a reader who evaluates behavioural-science
or SBCC talent for a living: this person can take a real, sourced gap in a market
leader, name the underlying behavioural mechanism correctly, and design an
intervention that targets the mechanism rather than the symptom. The product idea
(a personalised barcode scanner) is the vehicle. The proof of thinking is the point.

## 2. Target employer audience

Hiring managers and consultants evaluating applied behavioural-science or SBCC
capability — people who will judge this on whether the frameworks are used
correctly and the evidence is honestly graded, not on whether the app is buildable
or fundable. Two failure reads to actively avoid: (a) reading as a product pitch
with behavioural vocabulary sprinkled on top, (b) reading as a student exercise that
doesn't acknowledge its own evidence limits.

## 3. Main case study argument

Structure, following the design doc's Recommended Approach (B, opened with C's
hook):

1. **Diagnostic hook.** Yuka's own published scoring method (60% nutrition, 30%
   additives, 10% organic; identical for every user) is described neutrally, from
   Yuka's own documentation — not as a flaw, as a design fact with one structural
   consequence: it has no input for an individual's dietary constraint.
2. **Behavioural diagnosis.** COM-B (Michie et al. 2011) shows a generic score
   solves Capability and Opportunity but not Motivation-in-context. Dual-process
   theory (Kahneman; Evans & Stanovich) names why: the shopper is running slow,
   deliberative label logic under fast, low-attention shopping conditions, and a
   generic score doesn't remove that step. Cognitive load theory (Sweller)
   explains why the cost compounds as constraints stack.
3. **Intervention design.** Each product feature maps 1:1 to the mechanism it
   targets — personalised verdict removes the translation step; plain-language
   "why" addresses the interviewees' stated desire to understand the flag, not
   just receive a score.
4. **Evidence.** The verified contradiction (Warburtons Wholemeal Medium: good for
   a high-fibre goal, avoid for low-FODMAP) made concrete and interactive, plus the
   four interviews as the demand signal, framed exactly as strong as they are.
5. **Scope & limitations.** Sustainability is named as an explicit out-of-scope cut
   for data-reliability reasons, not a claim it sits outside the mechanism.

### User journey & emotional arc (resolved in /plan-design-review)

| Step | Reader does | Reader feels | Design must do |
|------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| 1. Landing | Reads proposition + hook | Skeptical curiosity → "this is a specific point, not a pitch" | Sharp, restrained hero (per DESIGN.md); no generic pitch copy |
| 2. Behavioural diagnosis | Reads COM-B + dual-process | Credibility building — risk of feeling lectured | Keep terse and example-anchored: name the mechanism, then immediately ground it in the interviewees' actual words, not a standalone framework explainer |
| 3. Evidence / interactive comparison | Toggles the two-profile verdict flip | **The emotional peak of the page** — surprise/delight at seeing the same product flip verdicts | Gets the most generous layout treatment on the page (DESIGN.md's staged full-width "stage" moment) — nothing else should compete with it for visual weight |
| 4. Scope & limitations | Reads what was excluded and why | Trust reinforcement — "they're not overselling this" | Plain, unhedged statement of exclusions (already specified in Section 6/7) |
| 5. Evidence appendix (if visited) | Checks a citation | Verification satisfaction — "I checked, it holds" | Anchor jump-and-return per the Section 5 nav resolution above |

**Design intent:** step 3 is the single moment the whole page is built to deliver.
Steps 1 and 2 exist to earn the reader's attention to get there; step 2 in
particular must stay tight — it is the section most tempting to over-write
(it's the most citation- and jargon-dense), and over-writing it risks the exact
failure mode Section 2 already names as something to avoid: reading as a
student exercise rather than applied insight.

## 4. Minimum interactive demo

**One interactive comparison, one product, two profiles.** A reader can toggle
between "High-fibre goal" and "Low-FODMAP (IBS)" against a single fixed product
(Warburtons Wholemeal Medium, GTIN 5010044000404) and see the verdict flip, with
the reasoning shown alongside (fibre content and the >=6g/100g claim threshold vs.
Monash's high-FODMAP classification of wholemeal wheat bread). Fully static: the
product data and both verdicts are pre-computed and hard-coded at build time. No
barcode input, no search, no third profile. The interaction is the argument made
tangible, not a product demo.

### Evidence taxonomy ↔ design system mapping (resolved in /plan-design-review)

`EVIDENCE-PACK.md`'s register grades every claim fully / partly / unsupported.
DESIGN.md's citation-colour system (the site's signature evidence-status
device) uses verified / inferred / excluded for the identical three-tier idea.
These are a direct 1:1 relabel, not two different taxonomies:

| EVIDENCE-PACK.md grade | DESIGN.md colour |
|---|---|
| Fully supported | Verified — sage `#4B6B53` |
| Partly supported | Inferred — ochre `#A67C2E` |
| Unsupported | Excluded — grey `#8A8578` |

Whoever implements the citation tags uses `EVIDENCE-PACK.md`'s register as the
source of truth for which grade each claim gets, then applies this table to
pick the colour. No claim should be graded twice with different outcomes
across the two documents.

### Interaction states (resolved in /plan-design-review)

- **Default/initial state:** both verdicts render visible at once (side by side
  on desktop, stacked on mobile — see Pass 6), each clearly labelled with its
  profile name. Nothing is hidden behind a click. The contradiction — the
  single strongest piece of evidence on the page — is visible to a reader who
  never interacts at all.
- **Toggle behaviour:** the toggle/tabs let a reader focus on one profile at a
  time (e.g. highlight or expand one side), but this is a refinement, not a
  reveal — no state exists where zero verdicts are shown.
- **No-JS fallback:** since both verdicts are pre-computed static data (no live
  computation), the no-JS case is simply the default state rendered without any
  toggle interactivity — both verdicts stay visible, statically, with no
  degraded or broken appearance. No separate no-JS design is needed.
- **Loading/error states:** not applicable — no network calls, no async data
  (per Fixed Exclusions: no live API calls, all data hard-coded at build time).

### Accessibility & responsive floor (resolved in /plan-design-review)

Full specification lives in DESIGN.md (Evidence-Status Colour Coding, Mobile &
Touch Behaviour, Keyboard & Focus, and the Motion section's
`prefers-reduced-motion` note); summarised here since it's a real build
requirement, not a nice-to-have:

- Evidence-status tags carry a text label alongside colour (never colour-only).
- Citation source popover is tap-triggered on touch, hover-triggered on desktop.
- Evidence appendix table restructures to stacked cards below ~600px.
- 44x44px minimum touch target on the toggle and citation tags.
- Toggle and citation tags are real keyboard-operable controls with a visible
  `:focus-visible` state.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` disables all transition animation; content/function
  is unaffected.
- Mobile (<768px): the sticky progress rail collapses to a thin top progress
  bar rather than disappearing.
- Excluded (grey) citation tags are interactive like verified/inferred tags —
  tapping shows the scope-cut's one-line rationale, not a traditional source.
- Default display order in the side-by-side comparison: High-fibre goal on the
  left/first, Low-FODMAP (IBS) on the right/second (matches the order both
  profiles are introduced in Section 4 above) — no meaningful tradeoff either
  way, so this is a default rather than a reviewed decision.

## 5. Website sections

1. **Landing.** One-line proposition + the diagnostic hook, linking straight into
   the case study.

   **Hero execution floor (resolved in /plan-design-review):** the flat
   warm-paper background and absence of imagery (per DESIGN.md's minimal
   decoration level) is a deliberate choice, not an omission — but it only
   works if the headline itself carries enough visual weight to be the page's
   one strong anchor. Concretely: the headline renders at DESIGN.md's full
   `clamp(2.5rem, 5vw, 4rem)` scale as a full-bleed-width text block (not a
   small, centred headline surrounded by empty space), in Instrument Serif at
   confident weight/contrast against the paper background. "Minimal" governs
   decoration, not scale or confidence — the hero must read as a deliberate
   typographic statement, not an empty page that forgot its hero.
2. **Main case study page.** The five-part argument above (Section 3), with the
   interactive comparison (Section 4) embedded at the Evidence step.
3. **Evidence & methodology appendix.** The evidence register (fully / partly /
   unsupported claims, per `EVIDENCE-PACK.md`), sources with reliability grading,
   and the interview methodology note (Section 6 of `PUBLIC-INTERVIEW-SUMMARY.md`).
   This is what makes the "not a hypothesis" claim defensible under scrutiny.
4. **About/contact.** Standard portfolio footer content — out of scope for this
   document.

No other pages. No blog, no multi-case-study index — this is a single-project site
per the design doc's Distribution Plan.

### Site structure & navigation (resolved in /plan-design-review)

Single page, no separate URLs. The Evidence & Methodology appendix is an
**anchored section at the bottom of the same page**, not a standalone route.
Every inline citation tag (`[S5]`, `[P02]`, etc.) in the case-study narrative is a
real in-page anchor link to its exact row in the appendix table; the appendix
itself carries a "back to case study" affordance so the jump is round-trip, not
one-way. This keeps the site a single document (no nav complexity for a
single-serving portfolio piece) while still making the citation-as-navigation
device concrete rather than decorative.

  5. Scope & limitations

  - Sources with reliability grading
  - Interview methodology note
  <-- "back to case study" link jumps to citation's origin point
  |
  v

## 6. Fixed exclusions

Locked scope; treat any of these appearing in a later draft as scope creep to flag,
not to quietly build:

- One static website only — no server-rendered app.
- One main case study page — no secondary case studies.
- One verified product example — no second or third contradiction demo.
- Two user profiles — no persona picker beyond high-fibre and low-FODMAP.
- One interactive comparison — no free-text barcode entry, no live scanning.
- No login or accounts.
- No backend.
- No live API calls (Open Food Facts, USDA, or otherwise) — all product data is
  pre-sourced and hard-coded, per Section 4.
- No real barcode scanner or camera access.
- No sustainability/environmental scoring, in any form.
- No medical recommendation claims, anywhere on the site.

## 10. Architecture (resolved in /plan-eng-review)

- **Stack:** plain HTML/CSS/vanilla JS. No framework, no bundler, no build
  step. Chosen over Astro (islands) and over adding a page to an existing
  site — this is a new, standalone, single-file-ish static project.
- **Evidence data — single source of truth:** the Evidence & Methodology
  section's HTML *is* the source of truth — no separate JS data file. Each
  entry in that section carries its citation ID, status, claim, and source
  title/link in semantic HTML and/or data attributes (e.g.
  `<tr id="s5" data-status="verified">...`). Inline citation tags in the
  narrative (`[S5]`) are anchor links into that section; JavaScript reads the
  existing DOM entries to power the tap/hover popover (Pass 6.2) rather than
  duplicating the data anywhere. This also means the full source list is
  fully readable with JavaScript disabled — the no-JS case degrades to "the
  citation is a normal link to the full entry," not a broken popover.
- **Verdict logic:** the two profile verdicts are generated by a small pure
  function, `computeVerdict(product, profile)`, taking the product's actual
  nutrition data and each profile's threshold rule (fibre ≥6g/100g claim
  threshold; Monash high-FODMAP classification) as inputs, unit-tested
  against both profiles for the Warburtons product plus boundary-value cases
  (e.g. fibre exactly at the 6g/100g threshold). The verdict text is
  generated output, not hand-typed prose, precisely because this one example
  is the site's central credibility claim.
- **Hosting/distribution:** Netlify, git-connected to this repo, deployed on
  push to main. No CI build step required (no bundler), Netlify serves the
  static files directly.
- **JS file organization (resolved in /plan-eng-review Code Quality pass):**
  `js/verdict.js` exports `computeVerdict()` with zero DOM dependency, directly
  importable into a test file with no browser required. `js/comparison.js`
  (toggle behaviour) and `js/citations.js` (popover behaviour, reading the
  appendix DOM per the data-source-of-truth decision above) handle rendering
  and import `verdict.js` where needed. Pure logic stays cleanly separable
  from DOM-wiring code.

the site's central credibility claim, so it gets full coverage, not a
happy-path spot check.
